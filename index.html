Jack Benarroch Jedlicki

I'm a first-year Computer Science PhD student at Harvard University, working on embodied intelligence.

Previously, I earned two bachelor's degrees in Mathematics and Physics at the University of Barcelona. During that time, I worked on computer vision and physics-informed neural networks at the Alaa Lab at UC Berkeley, on topology-informed generative models at the Computer Vision Center, and on tensor networks for efficient physics simulations at the Barcelona Supercomputing Center. Before that, I also worked on sensing and imaging techniques at MIT's Auto-ID Lab.

Research

I'm interested in deep learning, generative world models, and long-horizon reasoning for robotics.

Can a Frontier VLM Serve as a Robot Policy for Memory-Dependent Manipulation?
Jack B. Jedlicki, Amish Sethi, Heng Yang
arXiv preprint, 2026

Evaluates whether a frontier vision-language model, used directly as a robot policy, can solve manipulation tasks that require memory.

Where Memory Belongs: Ledger, an Object Ledger for Memory-Augmented VLAs
Tanguy Dieudonné, Jack B. Jedlicki, Heng Yang
NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Embodied Spatial Reasoning, 2026

Keeps short-term perceptual memory inside the policy and moves long-term object memory to an external, readable object ledger, lifting the four-suite average on RoboMME from 45.9% to 64.3%.

OCC4M: Object-Centric 4D Memory for Spatiotemporal Reasoning in Long-Horizon Manipulation
Jack B. Jedlicki, Tanguy Dieudonné, Heng Yang
IROS 2026 Workshop on Hybrid Architectures for Embodied Autonomy (Spotlight)
NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Embodied Spatial Reasoning, 2026

An object-centric 4D memory that tracks objects in a shared world frame along with their timing, motion, and containment, letting a VLM pick targets for a low-level controller without the full observation history.

A Unifying Lens on Reward Uncertainty in RLHF
Ely Hahami, Yoel Zimmermann, Ray Zhou, Jack B. Jedlicki
arXiv preprint, 2026

Distributional reward models that express uncertainty, showing that common ensemble aggregation rules (mean, worst-case, uncertainty-weighted) are special cases of a single expression.

Med-real2sim: Non-invasive medical digital twins using physics-informed self-supervised learning
Keying Kuang, Frances Dean, Jack B. Jedlicki, David Ouyang, Anthony Philippakis, David Sontag, Ahmed Alaa
NeurIPS, 2024

Generation of digital twins of the cardiovascular system using computer vision and physics-informed neural networks.

Topogen: topology-informed generative models
Jack B. Jedlicki
Mathematics Bachelor's Thesis, 2024

Introduces new methods for training generative models via differentiable topological regularizers, with novel proofs of differentiability and new connections between topology and generative modeling.

Tensor networks for quantum-inspired simulations
Jack B. Jedlicki
Physics Bachelor's Thesis, 2024

Study and analysis of tensor-network–based quantum-inspired simulators, demonstrating empirically that they can outperform classical algorithms in accuracy while using significantly less memory, enabling the solution of memory-intensive problems without quantum hardware.

Metal particle detection methods and their use for freeze-dried vaccine inspection: A review
Jack B. Jedlicki, Denise Tellbach, Brian Subirana
IEEE Sensors Journal, 2023

Sensing and imaging techniques for metal particle detection in freeze-dried vaccines.

Diffusion Policy: Is scaling all you need?
J. B. Jedlicki, K. Chattopadhyay
Final project for MIT 6.4212: Robotic Manipulation , 2025

Experimental study of the generalization and scaling behavior of diffusion policies under distribution shifts, including novel object configurations and obstacle-rich environments.

Are OT-aligned flows what you need?
J. B. Jedlicki, M. Letey, P. Nair, P. Nielsen, Y. Zimmerman
poster
Final project for Harvard CS 2840: Computational Optimal Transport for Machine Learning , 2025

Theoretical and experimental comparison of generative modeling approaches—including diffusion models, flow matching, and rectified flows—across image generation and robotic action generation tasks. We show that flow matching, unlike diffusion, enables one-shot inference for robot action trajectory generation.
